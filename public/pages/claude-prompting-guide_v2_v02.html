The difference between a vague request and a great one is rarely effort — it's structure. This guide distills the essentials of prompting Claude well, from six core principles to task-specific playbooks and fully worked examples.

01

General Principles

Six habits that make almost any prompt sharper. They compound — the best prompts use several at once.

1

Be clear and specific

State the task up front, give the context Claude needs, and break big jobs into steps.

Vague

"Help me with a presentation."

Effective

"Create a 10-slide deck for our quarterly sales meeting covering Q2 performance, top-selling products, and Q3 targets. Give me an outline with key points per slide."

WhyIt fixes the scope, purpose, and topics up front, so there's nothing left to guess.
2

Use examples

Show the output you want. A sample of the format, tone, or style is worth a paragraph of description.

Vague

"Write a professional email."

Effective

"Here's an email I sent before [paste]. Draft a new one in the same tone and structure, but for a one-month delay caused by supply-chain issues."

WhyA concrete sample gives Claude a reference point for tone and structure to match.
3

Encourage thinking

For complex tasks, ask Claude to work step-by-step and explain its reasoning.

Vague

"How can I improve team productivity?"

Effective

"Think step-by-step about improving my team's productivity: cover blockers, possible solutions, implementation challenges, and how to measure improvement — explaining your reasoning at each step, then summarize."

WhyA guided structure produces more accurate, detailed answers and a tidy summary to read.
4

Iterate and refine

If the first answer isn't right, give targeted feedback instead of starting over.

Vague

"Make it better."

Effective

"Good start — now make the tone more casual, add a customer-success example, and shorten paragraph two to focus on benefits over features."

WhySpecific adjustments let Claude make targeted edits rather than guessing what "better" means.
5

Leverage Claude's knowledge

Ask for explanations and background — but anchor the request with your context so the answer fits.

Vague

"What is marketing? How do I do it?"

Effective

"I'm building a marketing strategy for an eco-friendly cleaning line. Summarize current green-marketing trends: key messaging, effective channels, recent successful campaigns, and pitfalls like greenwashing."

WhyContext plus specific asks point Claude's broad knowledge directly at your situation.
6

Use role-playing

Assign a role or perspective to unlock more nuanced, expert-feeling responses.

Vague

"Help me prepare for a negotiation."

Effective

"Act as my fabric supplier. I want a 10% price cut. Give three objections you'd raise with counterarguments from my side, then two alternative offers — then switch roles and advise me as the buyer."

WhyA role surfaces multiple perspectives and sharpens the response to that viewpoint.
02

Task Playbook

The principles, applied. Each common task has a few levers that reliably raise quality.

A

Content creation

Three levers shape any piece of writing — who it's for, how it should sound, and how it's built.

Audience · who it's forTone & style · how it soundsStructure · how it's built
Vague

"Write something about cybersecurity."

Effective

"Outline a 1,000-word blog post on the top 5 cybersecurity practices for non-technical small-business owners. Plain language, practical and actionable, lightly humorous to keep interest."

WhyIt names the audience, tone, and shape — the three things generic prompts leave open.
B

Document summary & Q&A

Be specific about the focus, refer to documents by name, and ask for citations.

Specific · which aspectsName it · refer by titleCitations · point to sources
Vague

"Summarize this report for me."

Effective

"From 'Tech Industry Trends 2023', give a 2-paragraph summary on AI/ML trends, then answer: top 3 business AI applications, ML's impact on tech jobs, and adoption risks. Cite sections or page numbers."

WhyA defined focus, a named source, and a citation request make the answer targeted and verifiable.
C

Data analysis & visualization

Specify the output format precisely — the metrics, the sections, and how findings should be shown.

Vague

"Analyze our sales data."

Effective

"Analyze 'Sales Data 2023' and return: an executive summary, key metrics (quarterly totals, top category, highest-growth region), three trends, and three data-driven recommendations — then suggest three useful visualizations."

WhyNaming metrics and the exact layout turns a fuzzy ask into a repeatable, structured report.
D

Brainstorming

Ask for a defined number of ideas, set the parameters, and request a tidy format like a table or tagged list.

Vague

"Give me some team-building ideas."

Effective

"Brainstorm 10 virtual team-building activities for a remote team of 20. Note how each builds teamwork, tag them as ice-breaker / communication / problem-solving, and flag one low-cost and one premium option."

WhyBounds (count, categories, format) turn an open prompt into a structured, usable list.
03

Reliability

Three quick safeguards that reduce hallucinations and keep long or complex tasks on track.

Allow uncertainty

Tell Claude it's fine to say "I don't know." Permission to admit a gap is better than a confident guess.

Break down complex tasks

If steps are getting skipped, split the job into smaller pieces and work through them one message at a time.

Include all context

Each conversation starts fresh — Claude won't recall earlier chats. Restate the context every new request needs.
