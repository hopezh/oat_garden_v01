NotebookLM's closest match is Claude Projects

A persistent workspace where you upload your sources into a knowledge base and hold focused conversations grounded in that material.

Q — What is the equivalent of Google's NotebookLM in the Claude ecosystem?
The direct analog is Claude Projects. Same core idea: a self-contained workspace built around your own documents.

01The shared concept

Both tools revolve around grounding answers in your material rather than the model's general knowledge. In NotebookLM you create a notebook and add sources; in Claude you create a Project and populate its knowledge base.

Each Project keeps its own chat history, knowledge base, and custom instructions — isolated from the rest of your work, just as NotebookLM separates one notebook from another. You upload documents once and Claude references them automatically in every conversation inside that Project, so you never re-paste context.

You can also write Project instructions to tailor tone, output format, or have Claude answer from a specific role's perspective — the equivalent of steering how a notebook responds.

03Where they differ

NotebookLM leans into two signature features that Claude Projects does not replicate one-to-one:

Audio Overviews

NotebookLM can generate a podcast-style audio conversation about your sources. Claude Projects has no built-in audio or podcast generation.

Citation-anchored answers

NotebookLM's hallmark is linking each claim back to the exact source passage in its interface. Projects doesn't offer that same automatic inline source-passage citation UI.

If those two behaviors are your main reason for using NotebookLM, there isn't a precise equivalent in Claude today.

04What Projects gives back

In exchange for those specialized features, Projects is far more general-purpose. You're not limited to querying sources — you can also draft documents, write and debug code, analyze data, and iterate on outputs, all while using your uploaded material as grounding context.

So if your goal is "chat with my documents in a persistent, organized workspace," Projects is the direct analog — and it does considerably more besides.

06For your research work

For building science and solar work, a Project is a natural fit for keeping a single queryable workspace: drop in Radiance documentation, simulation methodology papers, and your own notes, then ask questions or generate analysis against all of it without re-uploading each time.

A tidy pattern: split materials into separate files by purpose (e.g. methodology · tool docs · prior cases) so retrieval stays focused and content doesn't get mixed.

07Setup in four steps

Create a ProjectIn Claude, open Projects in the left sidebar and start a new one. Name it by purpose, e.g. "Daylight Simulation — Project A".
Upload your sourcesAdd documents to the knowledge base via the + button — prefer well-structured PDF / DOCX / Markdown files.
Write instructionsSet tone, output structure, and rules (e.g. "cite the source file" / "don't fabricate data") so every chat inherits them.
Verify & chatAsk a check question to confirm the knowledge base is active, then work inside the project — drafting, querying, or analyzing.
